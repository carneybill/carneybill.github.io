The problem

Most small businesses do not fail at PPC because they need more dashboards. They fail because conversion tracking is thin, lead quality is unclear, budgets drift toward platform defaults, and campaign decisions are made too late or with too little context.

The platforms optimize for the signal they are given. If the signal is a cheap form fill instead of a qualified lead, booked job, sale, or profitable customer, the budget learns the wrong lesson.

Who it is for

Owner-led businesses, local service companies, niche ecommerce brands, consultants, clinics, B2B services firms, and lean teams that spend enough on paid media to need active management but do not want a bloated agency process.

Best fit when the business has a clear offer, a real conversion event, and the discipline to review lead quality instead of judging PPC only by clicks and form fills.

This is supervised automation, not black-box budget control.

The agent can monitor, diagnose, recommend, and apply approved low-risk changes. Bigger moves stay gated: new campaigns, major budget increases, targeting shifts, offer changes, and creative direction require human approval.

Setup sprint

Account and tracking audit

Review Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4, tags, pixels, landing pages, conversion events, UTM discipline, CRM handoff, and where lead quality data does or does not flow back into the ad platforms.

Conversion taxonomy

Define which events matter: qualified lead, booked call, purchase, quote request, consultation, repeat customer, offline sale, revenue value, and disqualified lead reasons.

Company memory

Build the agent's working memory: offers, margins, geography, customers, exclusions, seasonality, service areas, proof points, brand constraints, winning claims, and bad-fit traffic.

Budget guardrails

Set daily and monthly caps, change thresholds, pacing rules, approval requirements, stop-loss rules, and what the agent may change without asking first.

Optimization rules

Create first-pass rules for search terms, negatives, creative fatigue, cost-per-lead drift, conversion-rate drops, landing-page mismatch, audience waste, and campaign budget reallocation.

Reporting view

Create a practical weekly view of spend, conversions, conversion quality, CPA, ROAS or revenue proxy, budget pacing, experiments, and recommended next actions.

How the agent operates

  1. Observe: pull campaign, ad set, keyword, search term, creative, landing-page, and conversion data.
  2. Interpret: compare performance against company memory, budget limits, lead quality, and prior tests.
  3. Recommend: explain what should change, why it should change, expected risk, and what evidence supports the move.
  4. Execute: apply approved low-risk changes or prepare high-risk changes for review.
  5. Learn: write decisions, outcomes, exclusions, and performance lessons back into the account memory.

Monthly retainer

Weekly optimization review

Review spend, conversion quality, search terms, creative fatigue, budget pacing, landing-page performance, and recommended changes.

Campaign hygiene

Keep negatives, placements, underperforming creative, budget leakage, tracking issues, and account clutter from quietly draining performance.

Experiment planning

Plan controlled tests for offer angle, geography, audience, keyword theme, landing page, creative, bid strategy, and budget allocation.

Lead-quality feedback loop

Connect sales or owner feedback back to campaign decisions so the agent does not reward cheap leads that waste time.

Budget pacing

Monitor spend against monthly targets and flag when the account is underspending, overspending, or starving the best-performing campaigns.

Decision log

Maintain a plain-English change history so the business knows what changed, when it changed, why it changed, and whether it worked.

What it will not do

It will not guarantee lower acquisition costs, invent conversion data that does not exist, spend without a cap, ignore platform policies, or replace strategy. The value is discipline: better signal, faster diagnosis, controlled execution, and a memory-backed learning loop.

Use this when paid media needs an operator, not another dashboard.

The first goal is not maximum automation. The first goal is to stop wasting budget, clarify what counts as a real conversion, and build a repeatable PPC operating system that gets smarter each week.
